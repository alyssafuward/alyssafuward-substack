It shows up in every one of the 8 topics — nowhere does it disappear. Strongest in Creative Practice and Career & Work; even at its weakest, in generic Coaching & Leadership tips, it’s still present in a meaningful share of posts. That breadth — not any one topic — is the real evidence for the tagline.

Correction made during coding A first pass scored AI & Building lowest of all (1.4), only crediting posts where Alyssa narrated her own doubt in the text. That missed how a lot of this writing actually works: the limitation belongs to the reader — “I don’t think I can code” — and a shipped build or a 7‑year‑old’s working app stands in as the proof, not a confession. Rescored with that rule, AI & Building rose to 2.14, in line with the rest of the archive.

“I help people bridge the gap between ‘I can’t’ and ‘I can’”

This isn’t just a nice line — it’s a measurable, dominant structure across 3.5 years and nearly every topic. 62% of all 87 posts contain a complete, textually-evidenced “I can’t” → “I can” narrative; it opens the archive in December 2022 and closes it in August 2026; and it appears in some measure across every single topic she writes in.

Method

Coded from full article text (not titles) against a 3-part scheme: Topic (8 fixed categories), a 1–5 scale from identity to practical application, and the “I can’t” → “I can” narrative (named limitation + turning point + demonstrated capability, scored 0–3). Revised once mid-analysis: a limitation may belong to the implied reader rather than be self-narrated, and a working artifact counts as demonstrated capability. n = 87 published posts, alyssafuward.substack.com, Dec 2022–Aug 2026.
